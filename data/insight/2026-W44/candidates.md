# Insight lab 2026-W44: candidates (26 Oct 2026)

Sample: 244 domains found by web search (purposive, not random), 213 homepages scored, 24 failed, 7 skipped because robots.txt disallows our crawler. Scored by segment: SaaS 77, agency 66, professional services 70. Homepage only, one scan each, from a data-center address. The SaaS list was filled in largely from the search agent's own knowledge (see method.md), so it leans toward better-known companies.

Pipeline fix this run: `insight-scan.js` looked for the crawler state `blocked`, but the scanner reports `block`, so every site counted as non-blocking. Fixed in both scripts. With the fix, `analysis.json` counts 4 blocking sites; the names of the blocked crawlers were not recorded in this week's scans.

## 1. WordPress homepages score higher on readiness, and SEO plugin schema is almost the whole gap (confidence: medium)
- Claim: in our sample, WordPress homepages scored 6.9 points higher than homepages on every other platform, and the schema checks that SEO plugins fill by default explain about 5 of those points.
- Tension: practitioners expect newer stacks (Webflow, Next.js, custom builds) to be the cleaner, more machine-readable sites. On these checks, the older CMS wins, and the margin comes from defaults rather than effort.
- Evidence: WordPress 84.5 (n=93) vs others 77.6 (n=120), gap 6.9, bootstrap 95% interval 3.7 to 10.2. Within professional services: +8.9, interval 3.8 to 14.0 (39 vs 31). Within agencies: +6.8, interval 0.3 to 14.1 (45 vs 21, the second group under 30). Within SaaS: +5.8, interval -5.3 to 15.2 (9 vs 68; no conclusion). Organization/WebSite schema passes on 95% of WordPress vs 68% of the rest; content schema 73% vs 27%; JSON-LD present 97% vs 72%. The three schema checks give WordPress 14.1 points on average vs 8.9 (+5.2). Crawler access goes the other way: 17.3 vs 19.0 of 26 points.
- Mechanism: a follow-up fetch of 20 random WordPress homepages from the sample found Yoast on 9 and Rank Math on 6 (15 of 20). All 9 Yoast homepages carry BreadcrumbList, which the scanner counts as content schema. So the plugin, not the site owner, earns most of the points. Guess, not shown: Webflow and custom builds lack an equivalent default, so schema exists only where someone wrote it.
- Caveats: platform detection is a pattern match on page source. WordPress is concentrated in agencies and professional services and SaaS is mostly not WordPress; the within-segment numbers cover that only partly. A BreadcrumbList on a homepage is a thin signal. It passes our check, and we do not know if any AI system weighs it. This is readiness only.
- Monday morning: if the site is on Webflow, a headless stack or custom code, view the source of the homepage and look for an Organization and a WebSite JSON-LD block. If neither is there, add both by hand (name, url, logo, sameAs). If the site is on WordPress, check that the SEO plugin's schema output is switched on and that the organization name and logo are filled in.
- Method note: 213 homepages of small and mid-size B2B SaaS, agency and professional-services sites found by web search, scanned 26 Oct 2026, one scan each; plugin check on 20 of the 93 WordPress homepages, same day.
- Kept: medium. Both groups are over 90 sites, the gap is over 5 points, the interval excludes zero overall and within the largest segment, and the mechanism has a direct check. Not high: purposive sample, one scan, the platform mix differs by segment.

## 2. "Limited" AI crawler access is common, deliberate blocking is rare (confidence: low; do not publish as a standalone)
- Claim: 60% of homepages fail our "AI crawler access" check, yet only 4 of 213 (1.9%) block any of the ten crawlers. The rest lose points to ordinary path rules such as `Disallow: /wp-admin/`.
- Evidence: 119 of 213 sites have all ten crawlers "limited" (a Disallow on some path) and none blocked; 83 are open to all ten. All 4 blocking sites name AI crawlers in robots.txt (no blocking came from a wildcard or CDN default; 0 sites carry the Cloudflare managed robots.txt). If the 7 sites that disallowed our own crawler also block AI crawlers, the rate is at most 11 of 220 (5%).
- Why low: it repeats the July study (219 sites: 2% of crawler-site pairs blocked, 80% limited) and adds no new mechanism. The "accidental blocking by CDN or CMS defaults" question cannot be tested: there are no accidental blockers to study.
- Note for the owner (method, not content): the access check takes about 13 of 26 points from a site whose only rule is a default admin-path Disallow. It is the largest points bucket in this sample (7.8 points lost on average) and mostly reflects harmless rules. Worth a look before any post calls it "the single fix that moves scores most".

## 3. Agencies score higher than professional-services firms (confidence: do not publish)
- Claim: agency homepages averaged 83.6 vs 79.1 for professional services, gap 4.5, interval 0.5 to 8.1.
- Why discarded: the gap is under 5 points (low by rule), and 14 of 79 agency sites (18%) were lost to a host bot challenge that answered HTTP 202 (see below), so the scored agencies are a filtered group. The difference largely tracks the WordPress share (68% of scored agencies, 56% of professional services), so it is candidate 1 again.

Discarded: "One in six small agency sites challenges crawlers at the host" because the 18 HTTP 202 challenge pages (14 agency, 4 professional services) came from shared-host bot protection, and a re-fetch of three of them minutes later returned normal pages. It depends on our data-center address and timing. One scan cannot separate it from a transient rate limit.
Discarded: "Technical and trust pillars move together (r = 0.67)" because both pillars contain schema checks and the platform effect in candidate 1 drives both. It is the same finding seen twice.
Discarded: "SaaS vs agency gap (-4.1, interval -8.4 to 0.2)" because the interval includes zero.
